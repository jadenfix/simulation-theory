\begin{abstract}
Arguments about whether an observer is simulated often move rapidly from an abundance claim to a numerical posterior. We isolate logically prior questions. Do the competing hypotheses induce different laws on the declared observations? What is the measure-bearing unit, and how is a centered observer sampled from it? Is that sampling rule invariant to representational refinements that leave the physical and observational process unchanged? Do repeated observations constitute repeated draws from the population-level latent variable whose prevalence is being inferred, or are they conditionally generated after one persistent latent draw? And are the latent prevalence and observation mechanism separately identifiable from the available views?

We give a compact collection of exact results. Observationally equivalent hypotheses have Bayes factor one almost surely on their common observation law. Splitting one measure-bearing latent component into positive-weight observational clones preserves every finite shared-latent observation law, while raw label counting can move an apparent category frequency arbitrarily close to zero or one. A normalized local rule additive under all finite rational refinements must equal ordinary probability weight on rational inputs. We then formalize self-location by a conditional sampling kernel: population count ratios become centered odds only under corresponding uniformity and world-weighting assumptions, and representation-only cloning is posterior-invariant when the parent's sampling mass is conserved. When one latent model is drawn once and an arbitrarily long transcript is generated conditional on it, the likelihood ratio between hypotheses differing only in mixture weights is a posterior-weighted convex combination of component weight ratios; within-world repetition is therefore not repeated hyperprior sampling. For a known finite observation channel, latent mixing weights are identifiable exactly when channel rows are affinely independent. When both prior and channel are unknown, we exhibit a row-stochastic one-view gauge and an explicit local continuum under mild interiority/nondegeneracy conditions. Inside this declared gauge orbit, two conditionally independent repeated observations generated through the same channel and sharing the same persistent latent collapse the ambiguity to label permutation under full-row-rank and positivity assumptions.

These results do not argue that reality is or is not simulated and do not select a unique anthropic theory. They establish an audit order that should precede quantitative simulation-style Bayes: observation model, measure-bearing units and self-location rule, representation invariance, sampling hierarchy, identifiability, then Bayesian update.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Bostrom's simulation argument is a trilemma rather than a direct empirical detection theorem \citep{bostrom2003}. Bostrom and Kulczycki later identified a mathematical non sequitur in one formula used in the original presentation and proposed two patches intended to preserve the argument's conclusion \citep{bostromkulczycki2011}. Subsequent debate has focused on indifference, reference classes, self-locating evidence, observer abundance, and the probabilistic structure connecting simulated and unsimulated observers \citep{weatherson2003,crawford2013,franceschi2014,richmond2017,fallislewis2023}. More recent Bayesian treatments emphasize model uncertainty or conditional expected simulant ratios rather than a naively inserted realized population ratio \citep{kipping2020,thomas2024}.

The present paper asks a question logically prior to the choice of posterior formula: \emph{what statistical experiment is the observer participating in?} Data cannot point-identify a mixture parameter when distinct parameter values induce the same declared observation law; repeated observations conditional on one world are not automatically repeated samples from a prior over worlds; and a latent parameterization is not evidence merely because it contains more labels. A population count likewise becomes a self-locating probability only after one states how centered possibilities are weighted. These points are elementary in isolation. Their relevance to anthropic simulation inference is that a numerical posterior can be highly sensitive to exactly these hidden modeling choices.

The organizing audit order is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{observation model}
&\to\text{measure/self-location rule}\to\text{representation invariance}\\
&\to\text{sampling hierarchy}\to\text{identifiability}\to\text{Bayesian update}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is not offered as a replacement for SSA, SIA, full non-indexical conditioning, or other self-locating frameworks. Elga gives a canonical defense of indifference among certain subjectively similar centered possibilities \citep{elga2004}; Weatherson and Bostrom disagree about the role and formulation of indifference in the simulation argument \citep{weatherson2003,bostrom2005}; and Lewis and Fallis show directly that the treatment of self-location can materially change simulation conclusions \citep{fallislewis2023}. Neal's full non-indexical conditioning instead emphasizes conditioning on the observer's full evidence \citep{neal2006}. Our claim is narrower: whichever downstream self-locating rule one adopts, it should appear explicitly enough in the generative model that one can distinguish a changed sampling rule from new evidence.

\paragraph{Contributions and non-contributions.}
The individual mathematical ingredients below overlap with standard probability, finite-mixture identifiability, nonnegative-factorization nonuniqueness, and multiview latent-variable theory \citep{allman2009,gillis2020}. We do not claim novelty for finite additivity, elementary Bayes decompositions, ordinary affine-rank identifiability with known channel, or generic nonnegative-matrix-factorization ambiguity. The intended contribution is the integrated identifiability-first framework for simulation-style anthropic inference, together with explicit boundary examples and an executable audit artifact. In particular, the self-location kernel introduced below is not proposed as a new normative theory: it is a bookkeeping device that makes the probabilistic consequences of a chosen self-location rule explicit.

Lack of point identification does not make Bayesian analysis impossible. Robust Bayesian methods for set-identified models can report posterior lower and upper probabilities, sets of posterior means, or robust credible regions rather than treating a nonidentified structural quantity as data-determined \citep{giacomini2021}. Our claim is therefore not that Bayes must stop at nonidentification. It is that a single precise posterior over a nonidentified latent quantity necessarily reflects additional identifying restrictions, prior commitments, or sensitivity conventions that should be visible in the argument.

\section{Observable equivalence comes first}
Let $Y$ denote the complete transcript available to the inference procedure, and let $\mathcal F_Y$ be the sigma-field generated by that declared transcript. Let $P_B$ and $P_S$ be the laws induced on $(Y,\mathcal F_Y)$ by base and simulation hypotheses.

\begin{theorem}[Observable-equivalence ceiling]\label{thm:equiv}
If $P_B=P_S$ on $\mathcal F_Y$, every $\mathcal F_Y$-measurable statistic has the same law under the two hypotheses. Moreover,
\[
\frac{dP_S}{dP_B}=1
\qquad P_B\text{-almost surely}.
\]
Thus in a discrete or commonly dominated representation, wherever the common probability mass or density is positive,
\[
\mathrm{BF}_{S:B}(y)=1.
\]
Under equal priors, no classifier measurable with respect to $\mathcal F_Y$ has accuracy exceeding $1/2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equality of measures is preserved by measurable pushforward, so every measurable statistic has the same induced distribution. Equality also makes $P_S$ absolutely continuous with respect to $P_B$ with Radon--Nikodym derivative one $P_B$-almost surely. In a discrete or dominated representation this is the usual likelihood-ratio statement on common positive support. For equal-prior binary testing, optimal accuracy is $(1+\operatorname{TV}(P_B,P_S))/2=1/2$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is deliberately interface-relative. A restricted simulator architecture can be testable when it predicts a law different from ordinary physics on an allowed measurement. Conversely, an implementation distinction stipulated to preserve the entire declared observation law is not detectable from that law. This is a statistical statement, not a claim of metaphysical identity.

\section{Representation invariance and counting}
Let latent component $i$ have probability weight $w_i$ and conditional observation law $K_i$.

\begin{definition}[Observational refinement]
An observational refinement replaces one representation $(w,K)$ by finitely many labels
\[
(w_1,K),\ldots,(w_r,K),\qquad w_j>0,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^r w_j=w,
\]
where the labels are stipulated to be alternative descriptions of the same measure-bearing physical state, not additional independently realized observers or observer-moments.
\end{definition}

This distinction matters. \emph{Physical duplication} may legitimately increase whichever measure a theory assigns to newly created observers. The refinement operation here is only a redescription that preserves total weight and all conditional laws.

\begin{theorem}[Shared-latent refinement invariance]\label{thm:clone}
An observational refinement preserves the one-view law and every finite law of conditionally independent observations sharing the same latent component.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For one view, the refined contribution is
\[
\sum_j w_jK=wK.
\]
For $T$ conditionally independent views sharing the same latent component, it is
\[
\sum_jw_jK^{\otimes T}=wK^{\otimes T}.
\]
All other components are unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Raw label counting is representation-sensitive]\label{ex:clone}
Start with one base and one simulated latent label, each with probability weight $1/2$. Uniform counting over labels reports simulated fraction $1/2$. Replace only the simulated label by $r$ observational clones. Uniform label counting becomes
\[
\frac{r}{r+1}\to1.
\]
Instead clone only the base label and the count becomes
\[
\frac{1}{r+1}\to0.
\]
The observable process and total additive probability mass are unchanged.
\end{example}

The example is related in spirit, though not identical, to Everettian discussions of branching indifference and branch counting \citep{wilson2013,dizadji2015,khawaja2026}. It also sits near debates over subjectively identical or anomalous observer representations, including simulations, replays, and stored descriptions \citep{schneiderolum2013}. We do not infer a theory of consciousness from this. The narrower lesson is that a counting rule requires a principled individuation rule if mere redescription can change its output.

\begin{theorem}[Rational refinement-additive uniqueness]\label{thm:add}
Let $\mu:[0,1]\cap\mathbb Q\to\mathbb R$ satisfy $\mu(1)=1$ and finite split additivity: whenever rational $w_j\ge0$ satisfy $\sum_jw_j=w$,
\[
\mu(w)=\sum_j\mu(w_j).
\]
Then
\[
\mu(w)=w
\]
for every rational $w\in[0,1]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First split $0=0+0$. Additivity gives $\mu(0)=2\mu(0)$, hence $\mu(0)=0$. For $n\ge1$, split $1$ into $n$ equal rational pieces. Additivity gives $1=n\mu(1/n)$, hence $\mu(1/n)=1/n$. For positive $w=p/n$, sum $p$ such pieces to obtain $\mu(p/n)=p/n$.
\end{proof}

This theorem is elementary and is not offered as a novelty claim. A measure using consciousness, computational cost, causal depth, observer-moments, or other structure may differ from raw probability weight. The audit requirement is simply that the extra structure be stated and that representation-only refinements not silently create measure.

\section{Self-location as an explicit sampling kernel}\label{sec:selflocation}
A count ratio does not enter Bayes by itself. It enters through a rule connecting an uncentered world to a centered possibility. We make that rule explicit without taking a position on which self-location principle is normatively correct.

Let $W$ be a finite set of worlds with prior $\rho(w)$. In world $w$, let $C_w$ be a finite set of candidate centered possibilities, such as candidate observers or observer-moments. Let
\[
s(c\mid w)\ge0,\qquad \sum_{c\in C_w}s(c\mid w)=1,
\]
be a declared \emph{self-location kernel}. Let $L(e\mid w,c)$ be the likelihood of the observer's non-self-locating evidence $e$ conditional on world $w$ and centered possibility $c$. Finally let $F_w,G_w\subseteq C_w$ be disjoint centered categories, for example simulant and non-simulant candidates.

\begin{proposition}[Self-location kernel decomposition]\label{prop:selflocation}
For any evidence value with positive denominator probability,
\[
\frac{P(F\mid e)}{P(G\mid e)}
=
\frac{\sum_w\rho(w)\sum_{c\in F_w}s(c\mid w)L(e\mid w,c)}
{\sum_w\rho(w)\sum_{c\in G_w}s(c\mid w)L(e\mid w,c)}.
\]
Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
\item If a world $w$ is fixed, $s(c\mid w)=1/|C_w|$, and the relevant evidence likelihood is constant over $C_w$, then the within-world centered odds are $|F_w|/|G_w|$.
\item For a nonuniform kernel, the corresponding within-world odds are the ratio of total kernel masses $s(F_w\mid w)/s(G_w\mid w)$, not the raw count ratio.
\item With multiple uncertain worlds and uniform-within-world kernels, nondiscriminating evidence gives aggregate odds
\[
\frac{\sum_w\rho(w)|F_w|/|C_w|}{\sum_w\rho(w)|G_w|/|C_w|},
\]
which need not equal the raw ratio $\sum_w|F_w|/\sum_w|G_w|$.
\item If one centered possibility is replaced by observationally identical representational clones and its original kernel mass is split among those clones, every posterior quantity above is unchanged. Replacing that mass-conserving split by a newly uniform distribution over the refined labels changes the kernel and therefore changes the probabilistic model.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The joint mass of $(w,c,e)$ is proportional to $\rho(w)s(c\mid w)L(e\mid w,c)$. Summing this joint mass over $c\in F_w$ and over $c\in G_w$ and taking the ratio gives the displayed identity. Under a fixed world, a uniform kernel, and constant likelihood over $C_w$, all common factors cancel and only cardinalities remain. The nonuniform statement follows by replacing cardinality by total kernel mass. Summing over worlds gives the third expression. For refinement, replacing a term $s(c\mid w)L(e\mid w,c)$ by clone terms with identical likelihood and kernel masses summing to $s(c\mid w)$ leaves every numerator and denominator unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Counts, world weights, and refinement]\label{ex:selflocation}
Consider two equally likely worlds. In the first, one of two candidate centers is in $F$; in the second, three of four candidate centers are in $F$. Assume uniform self-location within each world and nondiscriminating evidence. Then
\[
P(F)=\frac12\frac12+\frac12\frac34=\frac58,
\qquad
P(G)=\frac38,
\]
so the odds are $5/3$. Yet the raw global count ratio is $4/2=2$. If instead the two worlds receive prior weights proportional to their reference-class sizes, $1/3$ and $2/3$, the global raw count ratio $2$ is recovered. Now split the single $F$ center in the first world into three representational clones. Assigning them masses $1/6,1/6,1/6$ conserves their parent's mass $1/2$ and leaves the overall odds $5/3$. Reimposing a uniform kernel over the four refined labels makes the first world's $F$ mass $3/4$ and changes the overall odds to $3$. No evidence changed; the self-location kernel did.
\end{example}

This formalization is deliberately neutral between competing theories of self-location. It makes precise one common form of indifference: uniformity of $s$ over a declared class of candidate centers. It also clarifies the scope of frequency calibration. Thomas's Calibration principle states, roughly, that when only reference-class membership and an $F:G$ ratio are given, centered odds track that ratio \citep{thomas2024}. In the finite model above, that behavior follows from a uniform kernel over the relevant candidate centers. The proposition does not refute Calibration; it exposes the generative assumption under which calibration is obtained and separates it from the additional problem of weighting different possible worlds. Similar distinctions motivate the long-standing debate over indifference and self-location \citep{elga2004,weatherson2003,bostrom2005,fallislewis2023}.

A physical global sampler need not literally exist for $s$ to be useful. The kernel is a representation of the conditional credence rule over centered possibilities. Conversely, full non-indexical conditioning need not be reduced to a uniform $s$: richer evidence is represented through $L(e\mid w,c)$, and another theory can use a different centered weighting. The point is to expose the assumption rather than settle it.

\section{Persistent latents and effective sample size}
Let $M$ be drawn once from a finite component set. Conditional on $M=m$, an arbitrary transcript $Y$ has law $P_m$. Assume either that $Y$ is discrete or that the component laws are dominated by one common $\sigma$-finite measure $\lambda$. Write $p_m(y)$ for the probability mass function in the discrete case or the Radon--Nikodym density $dP_m/d\lambda$ in the dominated case. For mixture weights $a=(a_m)$ and $b=(b_m)$, write
\[
p_a(y)=\sum_m a_mp_m(y),\qquad p_b(y)=\sum_m b_mp_m(y).
\]
In the dominated case, all density identities below are understood for the chosen Radon--Nikodym versions and therefore $\lambda$-almost everywhere. Consider hypotheses $H_a$ and $H_b$ that differ only in these mixture weights while sharing the conditional component laws $P_m$.

\begin{theorem}[Persistent-latent likelihood-ratio ceiling]\label{thm:persist}
Fix a transcript value $y$ with $p_b(y)>0$ in the discrete case, or a $\lambda$-almost-everywhere such value in the dominated case, and define the denominator-active set
\[
I_b(y)=\{m:b_mp_m(y)>0\}.
\]
Assume component-level absolute continuity on the realized support: whenever $a_mp_m(y)>0$, we also have $m\in I_b(y)$. Then
\[
\frac{p_a(y)}{p_b(y)}
=\sum_{m\in I_b(y)} P_b(M=m\mid y)\frac{a_m}{b_m}.
\]
Consequently the likelihood ratio lies between the minimum and maximum component ratios $a_m/b_m$ over denominator-active components with positive posterior weight.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The support assumption implies that every nonzero numerator contribution is indexed by $I_b(y)$. For $m\in I_b(y)$,
\[
\frac{a_mp_m(y)}{p_b(y)}
=\frac{b_mp_m(y)}{p_b(y)}\frac{a_m}{b_m}.
\]
The first factor is exactly $P_b(M=m\mid y)$. Summing over $I_b(y)$ gives the identity, and those posterior weights form a convex combination.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Support mismatch]
The finite ceiling is not universal. If some component has $a_mp_m(y)>0$ but $b_m=0$, then $H_b$ may assign zero density or mass to a transcript allowed by $H_a$, producing an infinite Bayes factor. This is outside Theorem~\ref{thm:persist}, not a contradiction of it.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[One persistent world is not one hundred world draws]\label{ex:persistent}
Draw $M\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\theta)$ once and set $Y_t=M$ for every $t$. Then
\[
P_\theta(Y_1=\cdots=Y_T=1)=\theta,
\]
not $\theta^T$. Comparing $\theta_a=3/4$ with $\theta_b=1/4$ gives Bayes factor $3$ after one observed $1$ or after one hundred repeated $1$s. The factor $3^{100}$ belongs to a different causal model in which $M$ is independently redrawn one hundred times.
\end{example}

A long within-world transcript can identify the active component $M$ extremely well. The theorem says something different: learning which component was selected is not the same statistical experiment as receiving repeated independent samples from the hyperprior over components.

\section{Known-channel mixture identification}
Let a finite row-stochastic observation channel have rows $K_1,\ldots,K_m\in\Delta_{d-1}$. A latent prior $\pi\in\Delta_{m-1}$ induces one-view law
\[
q=\pi K=\sum_{i=1}^m\pi_iK_i.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Known-channel affine-rank identifiability]\label{prop:affine}
For fixed known $K$, the map $\pi\mapsto\pi K$ is injective on the latent simplex if and only if the channel rows $K_1,\ldots,K_m$ are affinely independent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $\pi K=\pi'K$, then with $\delta=\pi-\pi'$ we have
\[
\sum_i\delta_iK_i=0,\qquad \sum_i\delta_i=0.
\]
Affine independence implies $\delta=0$, hence $\pi=\pi'$. Conversely, if the rows are affinely dependent, there exists nonzero $\delta$ satisfying both displayed equations. Choose an interior prior $u$ and sufficiently small rational or real $t>0$ so that $u\pm t\delta$ remain in the simplex. They are distinct priors but
\[
(u+t\delta)K=(u-t\delta)K.
\]
Thus the map is not injective.
\end{proof}

This is a standard finite-mixture geometry fact, included because it separates two questions that are often conflated: identifying $\pi$ when $K$ is known, and jointly identifying both $\pi$ and $K$ from observations.

\section{Joint prior/channel factorization and a one-view gauge}
When both $\pi$ and $K$ are unknown, observing $q=\pi K$ is a factorization problem. Nonnegative matrix and latent-variable models have a substantial literature on nonuniqueness and additional conditions required for identification \citep{allman2009,gillis2020}.

\begin{proposition}[One-view row-stochastic gauge]\label{prop:gauge}
Let $A$ be invertible, nonnegative, and row-stochastic. Define
\[
K'=AK,\qquad \pi'=\pi A^{-1}.
\]
If $\pi'$ is a valid probability vector, then $K'$ is row-stochastic and
\[
\pi'K'=\pi K.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Nonnegativity and row stochasticity of $A$ make each row of $K'$ a convex combination of channel rows, hence a probability law. Since $A\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1$ and $A$ is invertible, $A^{-1}\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1$, so $\pi'$ automatically sums to one; nonnegativity is the nontrivial validity requirement. Direct multiplication gives
\[
\pi'K'=\pi A^{-1}AK=\pi K.
\]
\end{proof}

The local-continuum claim can also be made explicit. Suppose $\pi$ is strictly positive and $K$ has at least two distinct rows. Choose a nonidentity row-stochastic matrix $B$ with $BK\ne K$ (for example, a permutation exchanging two distinct rows), and set
\[
A_t=(1-t)I+tB.
\]
For all sufficiently small $t>0$, $A_t$ is nonnegative, row-stochastic, and invertible; by continuity, $\pi A_t^{-1}$ remains strictly positive; and $A_tK\ne K$. Thus the gauge contains a genuine local continuum of distinct valid factorizations. The proposition itself is broader in one direction and narrower in another: it supplies a pointwise transformation whenever the transformed prior is valid, but it does not classify every possible stochastic factorization ambiguity.

\section{Repeated shared-latent views can change the experiment}
Suppose now that two observations are conditionally independent and identically distributed through the same channel $K$ given one persistent latent component. Their joint probability matrix is
\[
T=K^\top D_\pi K,
\]
where $D_\pi$ is diagonal with entries $\pi_i$. Independent redraws of the latent between the two observations would instead produce $q\otimes q$; these are different experiments.

\begin{theorem}[Two repeated-view rigidity inside the row-stochastic gauge]\label{thm:twoview}
Assume: (i) $K$ has full row rank; (ii) both observations use the same channel $K$ and are conditionally independent given the same persistent latent; (iii) $A$ is invertible, nonnegative, and row-stochastic; (iv) $K'=AK$; (v) $\pi'=\pi A^{-1}$ is strictly positive; and (vi) the repeated shared-latent two-view law is preserved:
\[
K'^\top D_{\pi'}K'=K^\top D_\pi K.
\]
Then $A$ is a permutation matrix.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substituting $K'=AK$ gives
\[
K^\top A^\top D_{\pi'}AK=K^\top D_\pi K.
\]
Let
\[
X=A^\top D_{\pi'}A-D_\pi.
\]
Then $K^\top XK=0$. Because $K$ has full row rank, there exists a right inverse $R$ with $KR=I$. Multiplying by $R^\top$ on the left and $R$ on the right yields
\[
X=R^\top K^\top XKR=0,
\]
so
\[
A^\top D_{\pi'}A=D_\pi.
\]
For distinct columns $j\ne k$, the $(j,k)$ entry is
\[
0=\sum_i\pi'_iA_{ij}A_{ik}.
\]
Every term is nonnegative and every $\pi'_i$ is strictly positive. Hence $A_{ij}A_{ik}=0$ for every row $i$ and every $j\ne k$: each row has at most one positive entry. Row stochasticity makes that unique positive entry equal to one. Invertibility prevents two rows from selecting the same column. Thus $A$ is a permutation matrix.
\end{proof}

The theorem is intentionally narrow. It does \emph{not} state that arbitrary two-view latent-class models are globally identifiable. It states that the continuous row-stochastic gauge of Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge} collapses to label switching when a second conditionally independent observation generated through the same channel and tied to the same persistent latent is also preserved, under the listed rank and positivity assumptions. General latent-structure identification uses additional tensor, rank, or structural conditions \citep{allman2009}.

\section{Relation to anthropic and simulation arguments}
Bostrom's classic argument links posthuman survival and simulation behavior to the fraction of observers with human-type experiences that are simulated \citep{bostrom2003}. The later patch paper is itself a useful warning that seemingly small probability-formula choices can matter even when the intended philosophical conclusion is retained \citep{bostromkulczycki2011}. Weatherson, Crawford, Franceschi, and Richmond challenge aspects of indifference, reference classes, evidence, or sampling assumptions \citep{weatherson2003,crawford2013,franceschi2014,richmond2017}. Elga supplies an influential defense of indifference for self-locating belief \citep{elga2004}. Lewis and Fallis focus directly on simulation and self-location \citep{fallislewis2023}. Kipping adds uncertainty about whether ancestor simulations are technically realizable and uses Bayesian model averaging \citep{kipping2020}. Thomas develops a conditional expected simulant-ratio argument and makes its self-location calibration principle explicit \citep{thomas2024}.

Our framework is complementary rather than substitutive. Model averaging still requires specified models and likelihoods. An expected simulant ratio still requires both a definition of the units whose measure is being aggregated and a rule connecting those units to centered credence. Proposition~\ref{prop:selflocation} provides a finite generative representation of that second object: Thomas-style count calibration is recovered under an appropriate uniform self-location kernel, while different world weighting produces different aggregate odds. This is a clarification of assumptions, not a rejection of calibration. Full non-indexical conditioning still requires a generative model under which the observer's complete evidence has a likelihood \citep{neal2006}. Robust Bayesian inference under set identification likewise requires an explicit identified set and an explicit treatment of prior ambiguity over nonidentified components \citep{giacomini2021}. The present audit asks whether those ingredients have stable statistical meaning before a posterior is evaluated.

The refinement result should therefore not be read as ``copy counting is always wrong.'' If a physical process creates additional conscious observers and a theory assigns them additional measure, the model has changed. The diagnostic is instead: if a transformation is explicitly declared to be representationally redundant and to preserve total measure-bearing structure, a posterior should remain invariant when the self-location mass is conserved. If it changes because one reimposes uniformity over a newly refined label set, the change arose from a changed sampling rule rather than from observational evidence.

\section{A nine-step pre-Bayes audit}
For a quantitative simulation-style inference, the following questions can be asked before reporting a posterior.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Hypothesis restriction.} What concrete base and simulator models are being compared? A completely unrestricted simulator class can contain models observationally equivalent to the base model.
\item \textbf{Observation interface.} What transcript or measurement sigma-field supplies the likelihood?
\item \textbf{Measure-bearing unit.} Are we counting persons, observer-moments, world-histories, computations, or something else?
\item \textbf{Self-location rule.} Conditional on a world, what kernel or credence rule weights the candidate centered possibilities? Does a population count enter because uniformity has been assumed, or for some other stated reason?
\item \textbf{Representation invariance.} Does an observationally null relabeling/refinement change the assigned probability when total measure and centered sampling mass are conserved? If so, what additional physical or phenomenal structure makes that change substantive?
\item \textbf{Sampling hierarchy.} Which latent variable is drawn once, and which quantities are independently redrawn across observations?
\item \textbf{Conditional identification.} If the observation channel is treated as known, are the latent mixture weights identifiable and sufficiently well-conditioned?
\item \textbf{Joint identification.} If both prior and channel are inferred, what additional views, interventions, restrictions, or invariances break factorization ambiguity?
\item \textbf{Anthropic update.} Only after the previous choices are fixed should one apply the selected ordinary or self-locating Bayesian rule, reporting identified sets or prior sensitivity rather than point certainty when point identification fails.
\end{enumerate}

These steps do not force agreement about anthropic probability. They expose where disagreement is mathematical, empirical, representational, or philosophical.

\section{Reproducibility and falsification policy}
The accompanying repository contains the manuscript source, bibliography, machine-readable claim ledger, synthetic peer-review record, field-impact literature audit, citation-provenance record, and a standard-library Python reproduction program using exact rational arithmetic for finite examples. The code checks examples and attempts to falsify theorem boundaries; it is not used as a substitute for the analytic proofs.

The reproduction suite includes: clone-count instability while additive weight remains fixed; an exact self-location example separating raw counts, world weighting, and mass-conserving representation refinement; persistent-world versus independent-redraw Bayes factors; a deliberate support-mismatch boundary; full-rank and rank-deficient known-channel examples; several exact one-view gauge transformations; and exhaustive rational-grid searches over bounded two- and three-state row-stochastic gauges for the two-view theorem. A finite search can catch algebra or implementation errors but cannot prove the general theorem.

The exact-rational `receipt.json` is required to regenerate byte-for-byte from `reproduce.py`. The PDF is treated differently: the release workflow records the TeX/Python toolchain and a release-specific PDF hash, but does not claim that PDF bytes must remain invariant across future TeX distributions or creation timestamps. The workflow must compile the PDF, fail on unresolved references or overfull boxes, preflight its metadata, and upload the complete release audit bundle. A human visual inspection remains a separate release checklist item.

\section{Limitations}
Several limitations are essential to the interpretation.

First, observational equivalence is relative to the declared interface; a richer experiment can distinguish models that a poorer interface cannot. Second, the refinement theorem concerns representation-only splitting, not physical creation of new observers. Third, the self-location kernel is a mathematical representation of a centered credence rule, not a claim that a physical global sampler exists and not a derivation of a uniquely rational anthropic principle. Raw count calibration follows only under the corresponding uniformity and world-weighting assumptions. Fourth, the persistent-latent ceiling compares hypotheses that share component transcript laws and differ in mixture weights; its displayed density form additionally assumes a discrete transcript or a common dominating measure and is interpreted almost everywhere. Changing the component likelihoods changes the problem. Fifth, known-channel affine-rank identifiability says nothing by itself about statistical conditioning or finite-sample efficiency, and nonidentification does not preclude set-valued or robust Bayesian analysis. Sixth, the row-stochastic gauge is a constructive nonidentifiability family, not a complete classification of nonnegative factorizations. Seventh, the two-view theorem is a same-channel repeated-view rigidity result inside that gauge orbit, not generic two-view latent-class identifiability. Finally, none of the theorems determines whether consciousness is substrate independent, whether advanced civilizations run simulations, or which self-locating probability rule is normatively correct.

\section{Conclusion}
The paper's organizing principle is an order of audit rather than a new posterior formula:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{observation model}
&\to\text{measure/self-location rule}\to\text{representation invariance}\\
&\to\text{sampling hierarchy}\to\text{identifiability}\to\text{Bayesian update}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Bayesian arithmetic is powerful after these objects are defined. It cannot by itself choose the unit of counting, turn a population cardinality into centered odds without a sampling rule, turn one persistent world into many independent draws from a world prior, or identify a latent factorization that the observable law leaves ambiguous.

For simulation arguments, the practical implication is modest but consequential. Before interpreting a numerical probability that ``we are simulated,'' one should be able to say what is being sampled, what carries measure, how self-location weights candidate observers, what observations distinguish the hypotheses, and whether the latent quantities entering the calculation are identifiable under that experiment. If point identification fails, a responsible Bayesian analysis can report posterior sensitivity, identified sets, or robust ranges; it should not present prior-dependent precision as if it were learned uniquely from the data. If these questions are unanswered, the posterior may be precise arithmetic applied to an underdetermined model.
