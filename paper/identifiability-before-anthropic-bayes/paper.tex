\begin{abstract}Arguments about whether an observer is simulated often move quickly from abundance claims to a posterior probability. We isolate prior questions: whether competing hypotheses induce different observable laws, whether an observer measure is invariant to representational refinements that change no observable process, and whether repeated evidence corresponds to repeated draws from the population-level latent variable whose prevalence is being inferred. Observationally equivalent hypotheses have Bayes factor one. Splitting a latent component into positive-weight observational clones preserves every finite shared-latent observation law while label counting can move apparent category probability toward zero or one; normalized local rational split-additivity recovers probability weight. When one latent model is drawn once and an arbitrarily long transcript is generated conditional on it, the likelihood ratio between hypotheses differing only in mixing weights is a posterior-weighted convex combination of component weight ratios, so within-world repetition is not repeated hyperprior sampling. We distinguish conditional mixture identifiability from joint prior/channel identifiability, exhibit a continuous one-view factorization gauge, and show that within this gauge two conditionally independent views sharing one persistent latent collapse the ambiguity to label permutation under rank and positivity assumptions. These results do not argue that reality is or is not simulated. They give identifiability and invariance conditions that should precede quantitative anthropic Bayes.\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
Bostrom's simulation argument is a trilemma rather than a direct empirical proof \citep{bostrom2003}. Discussion has focused on indifference, reference classes, self-location, and evidence for simulant abundance \citep{weatherson2003,crawford2013,franceschi2014,richmond2017,thomas2024}. We ask a logically prior question: what statistical experiment is the observer participating in?

A mixture parameter is not estimable before it is identifiable; repeated conditional observations are not automatically repeated samples from a population mixing distribution; and a latent parameterization is not evidence merely because it contains more labels. The same discipline applies to anthropic reasoning. Our ingredients connect to observation-selection theory \citep{bostrom2002}, simulation-argument critiques, Everettian branching debates \citep{wilson2013,dizadji2015,khawaja2026}, and latent-model identifiability \citep{allman2009}. We do not claim priority for those general literatures. The contribution is their integrated use as an auditable precondition hierarchy for simulation-style Bayes.
\section{Observable equivalence comes first}
Let $Y$ be the complete allowed transcript and $P_B,P_S$ the induced laws under base and simulation hypotheses.
\begin{theorem}[Observable-equivalence ceiling]\label{thm:equiv}If $P_B=P_S$, every measurable statistic of $Y$ has the same law. Wherever the common probability is positive, $\mathrm{BF}_{S:B}(y)=1$. Under equal priors no classifier based only on $Y$ exceeds accuracy $1/2$.\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}Equality is preserved by measurable pushforward; the likelihood ratio is one; and equal-prior optimal binary accuracy is $(1+\operatorname{TV}(P_B,P_S))/2$.\end{proof}
A restricted simulator architecture may be testable by predicting a different law. An implementation distinction stipulated to preserve the full allowed law is not detectable from that law.
\section{Representation invariance and counting}
Let latent component $i$ have weight $w_i$ and conditional law $K_i$. An \emph{observational refinement} replaces $(w,K)$ by $(w_1,K),\ldots,(w_r,K)$ with positive $w_j$ summing to $w$.
\begin{theorem}[Shared-latent refinement invariance]\label{thm:clone}Such refinement preserves the one-view law and every finite conditionally independent shared-latent view law.\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}$\sum_jw_jK=wK$, and for $T$ shared-latent views $\sum_jw_jK^{\otimes T}=wK^{\otimes T}$.\end{proof}
With one base and one simulated label of weight $1/2$, uniform label counting gives $1/2$. Clone only the simulated label $r$ times and it gives $r/(r+1)\to1$; clone only base and it gives $1/(r+1)\to0$. Nothing observable changed. This resembles, without being identical to, disputes over branching indifference and branch counting in Everettian probability \citep{wilson2013,dizadji2015,khawaja2026}: a count requires a principled individuation rule.
\begin{theorem}[Rational refinement-additive uniqueness]\label{thm:add}If $\mu:[0,1]\cap\mathbb Q\to\mathbb R$ has $\mu(1)=1$ and is additive under every finite rational split, then $\mu(w)=w$ for all rational $w\in[0,1]$.\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}Split $1$ into $n$ equal pieces, giving $\mu(1/n)=1/n$, then sum $p$ pieces.\end{proof}
This elementary theorem is diagnostic, not a novelty claim. Measures using consciousness, computation, causal depth, or other structure may differ, but that structure must be explicit.
\section{Persistent latents and effective sample size}
Let $M$ be drawn once. Conditional on $M=m$, transcript $Y$ has law $P_m$. Hypotheses $a,b$ differ only in mixing weights $a_m,b_m$.
\begin{theorem}[Persistent-latent likelihood-ratio ceiling]\label{thm:persist}For $y$, assume $b_m>0$ whenever $P_m(y)>0$. Then
\[\frac{P_a(y)}{P_b(y)}=\sum_mP_b(M=m\mid y)\frac{a_m}{b_m},\]
so the ratio lies between the relevant minimum and maximum $a_m/b_m$.\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}Expand both mixtures and identify $b_mP_m(y)/P_b(y)$ as the posterior weight.\end{proof}
The support assumption is essential. If the denominator excludes a component allowed by the numerator, a finite ceiling need not exist.
\begin{example}Draw $M\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\theta)$ once and set $Y_t=M$. Then $P_\theta(Y_1=\cdots=Y_T=1)=\theta$, not $\theta^T$. Comparing $3/4$ to $1/4$ gives Bayes factor $3$ after one or one hundred repeated ones. $3^{100}$ belongs to a model that redraws $M$ independently one hundred times.\end{example}
A long transcript can identify the active component; it is not thereby many samples from the hyperprior.
\section{Prior/channel factorization}
Let $K$ be a row-stochastic channel, $\pi$ a latent prior, and $q=\pi K$. Given known $K$, $\pi$ is identifiable iff the channel rows are affinely independent. Rank failure yields a nonzero zero-sum $\delta$ with $\delta K=0$. Broader latent-structure identifiability results use related rank/tensor conditions \citep{allman2009}.
\begin{proposition}[One-view row-stochastic gauge]\label{prop:gauge}Let $A$ be invertible, nonnegative, and row-stochastic. Set $K'=AK$, $\pi'=\pi A^{-1}$. Whenever $\pi'$ is a valid prior, $\pi'K'=\pi K$.\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}Direct multiplication. Row stochasticity preserves normalization and nonnegativity preserves channel rows.\end{proof}
Thus infinite one-view data may identify $q$ without identifying a unique $(\pi,K)$.
\section{Shared-latent views can change the experiment}
For two conditionally independent observations sharing one latent component, $T=K^\top D_\pi K$. The following is only a gauge-orbit result, not unrestricted two-view latent-class identifiability.
\begin{theorem}[Two-view rigidity inside the gauge]\label{thm:twoview}Assume $K$ has full row rank; $A$ is invertible, nonnegative and row-stochastic; $K'=AK$; $\pi'=\pi A^{-1}$ is strictly positive; and the shared-latent two-view law is preserved. Then $A$ is a permutation matrix.\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}Two-view equality yields $K^\top A^\top D_{\pi'}AK=K^\top D_\pi K$. A right inverse of full-row-rank $K$ gives $A^\top D_{\pi'}A=D_\pi$. For $j\ne k$, $0=\sum_i\pi_i'A_{ij}A_{ik}$. All terms are nonnegative and $\pi_i'>0$, so each row has at most one positive entry. Row stochasticity makes it one; invertibility makes selected columns distinct.\end{proof}
Independent latent redraws instead give $q\otimes q$. The causal coupling of observations, not merely their number, determines identification.
\section{Implications for simulation arguments}
A numerical simulation posterior should specify: (i) the restricted hypothesis that supplies a likelihood; (ii) the measure-bearing unit and its invariance under redundant representation; (iii) what is sampled once and what is resampled; (iv) whether latent prevalence and observation mechanisms are identifiable; and (v) which update is ordinary likelihood evidence versus self-locating conditioning.

Reference-class sensitivity is established in the anthropic literature \citep{bostrom2002,franceschi2014,richmond2017}. Refinement invariance adds a separate test: after choosing a reference class, duplicating a redundant description should not manufacture probability mass unless the duplication represents additional measure-bearing structure.
\section{Relation to recent simulation arguments}
Bostrom supplies the classic trilemma \citep{bostrom2003}; Weatherson challenges its indifference step \citep{weatherson2003}; Crawford argues that evidence that simulants exist need not establish that one is a simulant \citep{crawford2013}; Franceschi and Richmond emphasize reference-class sensitivity \citep{franceschi2014,richmond2017}.

Thomas's recent ``Simulation Expectation'' replaces a high realized simulant ratio with a high conditional expected ratio and uses a stipulated reference class of people in broadly similar worlds \citep{thomas2024}. Our framework is complementary. A high expected ratio can support a posterior after the units entering the ratio and the map from world structure to observer measure are fixed. Refinement invariance tests stability under observationally null redescription; persistent-latent semantics tests whether evidence is from one selected world or repeated world draws; factorization identifiability tests whether latent prevalence and observation channel can be separately recovered.
\section{Scope, reproducibility, and conclusion}
We do not show that Bostrom's trilemma is false, that simulation is unlikely, or that there is a privileged anthropic reference class. We do not claim probability weight is the only possible observer measure once additional physical or phenomenal structure is admitted. We do not claim two views identify arbitrary latent-class models. The novelty claim is modest: an integrated identifiability hierarchy applied to simulation-style anthropic Bayes, accompanied by an executable audit artifact.

The repository freezes this manuscript, its BibTeX database, a machine-readable theorem manifest, and a standard-library Python script that reproduces every numerical example in exact rational arithmetic and emits a deterministic JSON receipt. CI checks compilation of the audit code and all receipts. Passing CI is evidence that the artifact reproduces its declared finite calculations; it is not treated as proof of novelty or of a physical simulation hypothesis.

The order of operations is the central result:
\[
\boxed{\text{observation model}\to\text{representation/measure}\to\text{sampling hierarchy}\to\text{identifiability}\to\text{Bayesian update}.}
\]
Bayesian arithmetic is powerful after these objects are defined. It cannot by itself choose the unit of counting, turn one persistent world into many independent worlds, or identify a latent factorization that the observable law leaves ambiguous.
