# Identifiability Before Anthropic Bayes — reproducible paper artifact

Author: **Jaden Fix**, Tempera — `Jaden@Tempera.dev`

This directory is the source-of-truth bundle for the paper. The manuscript is a foundations/identifiability result, not a claim that reality is simulated. The individual elementary lemmas are not advertised as novel; the intended contribution is the integrated pre-Bayes audit framework and its application to simulation-style anthropic inference.

The paper's current hierarchy is:

```text
observation model
  -> measure-bearing unit and self-location rule
  -> representation invariance
  -> latent sampling hierarchy
  -> statistical identifiability
  -> Bayesian / self-locating update
```

The self-location step is explicit because a population count does not become centered odds without a rule connecting a world to candidate centered possibilities. Uniform counting is one possible conditional-kernel choice, not an observational datum and not evidence for a literal physical global sampler.

## Exact reproduction

```bash

```

The script uses `fractions.Fraction` for the finite calculations. It checks successful examples and failure boundaries, including:

- representation-only clone counting versus additive mass;
- self-location count calibration, world weighting, and mass-conserving refinement;
- persistent-world versus independent-redraw Bayes factors;
- support mismatch;
- rank-deficient mixture collisions;
- invalid transformed priors;
- bounded two- and three-state searches around the repeated-view rigidity theorem.

The finite searches are falsification aids. They do **not** replace the general analytic proofs in `paper.tex`.

## Self-location scope

The paper models centered uncertainty using a normalized conditional kernel `s(c|w)` over candidate centered possibilities in world `w`. This is a mathematical representation of a self-location credence rule. It is **not** a claim that a physical global sampler exists, and the paper does not select a uniquely correct kernel. This role is closely related to established xerographic-distribution work, Garisto's peer-reviewed observer-selection formalism, and Uchida's 2026 observer-measure specification manuscript; neither the location-distribution object nor observer-selection/measure role separation is claimed as new.

Under a fixed world, a uniform kernel over a declared reference class and nondiscriminating evidence recovers ordinary count calibration. Under the **same fixed-world, nondiscriminating-evidence condition**, a nonuniform kernel gives odds equal to the ratio of total self-location masses, provided the denominator mass is positive. If evidence likelihoods differ across candidate centers, the correct expression is likelihood weighted rather than the bare kernel-mass ratio.

Across uncertain worlds, raw global counts additionally require a world-weighting convention. Representation-only cloning leaves inference unchanged when the parent's kernel mass is split among the clones; reimposing uniformity after cloning is a different self-location model.

The field-impact and Rounds 9--10 audits compare this formulation directly with Thomas's Calibration principle, Hartle--Srednicki xerographic distributions and selection-fallacy warning, Garisto's observer-selection formalism, Elga-style indifference, the Weatherson/Bostrom exchange, Fallis--Lewis on simulation and self-location, Neal's FNC, Schneider--Olum's anomalous reference-class problem, and Uchida's contemporaneous measure-specification framework.

## Mathematical scope notes

The persistent-latent likelihood-ratio theorem is stated for either a discrete transcript or a family of component laws dominated by one common sigma-finite reference measure. For a finite component family such a dominating measure always exists, e.g. `lambda = sum_m P_m`; it is a device for a common density representation rather than an extra empirical restriction. In the dominated case the density identity is an almost-everywhere statement. `p_m(y)` denotes the corresponding probability mass or Radon--Nikodym density.

The one-view factorization proposition is pointwise: it applies whenever the transformed prior remains nonnegative. The manuscript separately constructs a genuine local continuum when the prior is interior and the channel has at least two distinct rows.

The repeated-view theorem assumes that both conditionally independent observations use the same channel `K` and share one persistent latent state. It is not a theorem for arbitrary view-specific channels. Lack of point identification also does not forbid Bayesian analysis: set-valued or robust posterior reporting remains available, but its prior and identifying restrictions must be explicit.

## Audit policy

1. Theorem proofs live in the manuscript rather than being delegated to code.
2. Code checks finite examples and boundary witnesses; no finite check is promoted into an unrestricted theorem.
3. `claims.json` distinguishes classical consequences, elementary structural lemmas, the elementary self-location Bayes decomposition, standard known-channel geometry, constructive witnesses, and the scoped gauge-orbit theorem.
4. The self-location proposition does not select a normative anthropic rule; count and mass calibration require the stated likelihood conditions, and the conditional-kernel/xerographic/observer-selection/observer-measure role is not claimed as new.
5. The repeated-view theorem is explicitly **inside the declared nonnegative row-stochastic gauge orbit**, assumes the same observation channel for both views, and is not a global two-view latent-class identifiability theorem.
6. The persistent-latent likelihood-ratio ceiling has explicit domination and component-level absolute-continuity/support requirements for its displayed pointwise form. Support mismatch is tested as a negative boundary case.
7. Representation refinement is distinguished from physically creating additional measure-bearing observers.
8. The refinement-additivity theorem is elementary; it is not presented as a new mathematical discovery.
9. Point nonidentification is not equated with the impossibility of Bayesian inference; identified-set and robust-prior methods are acknowledged.
10. A DOI is not treated as sufficient bibliographic verification. `citation_provenance.json` records the publisher/primary source and any discrepancy used during the paper audit.
11. Synthetic peer review is preserved but clearly labeled synthetic. External expert review remains necessary before claiming external peer validation.
12. CI success establishes reproducibility of the declared artifact; it does not establish novelty, metaphysical truth, or external peer acceptance.
